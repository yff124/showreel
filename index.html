How it was made

  1. One line of brief A silent morning ritual, three shots, one warm practical light against one cold window. Nothing more was written down before generating.
  2. Lock the look before spending a single render Palette, lens, light direction, grain and camera behaviour were written once and repeated verbatim in all four prompts. This is the whole reason consecutive shots read as the same world. It is the cheapest step and the one most people skip.
  3. Generate the shots Four text-to-video generations at 1080p, 24fps, each 5–6s, with the model's native audio kept. Shot list before prompts — always, otherwise you are just rolling dice and hoping two of them match.
  4. Cut it Hard cuts between shots, a 0.6s fade in from black and a 0.8s fade out, audio faded to match. Deliberately sparse — nothing is on the timeline that does not earn its place.
  5. Deliver for both frames 16:9 master plus a 9:16 cut composed separately, and stills pulled for thumbnails. One shoot, three placements.

AI disclosure, plainly. Every frame on this page is AI-generated, and I am the one who directed it. Nothing here is presented as live-action footage I shot, and no stock material was used.

No music bed, on purpose. I do not ship audio I cannot license. The ambience you hear comes with the generated clips. Where a brief needs a score, the track gets licensed first — the edit waits.
